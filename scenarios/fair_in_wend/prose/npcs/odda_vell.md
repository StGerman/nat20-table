# Odda Vell, the grain factor

**Who she is.** A broad, weathered woman of about fifty in a man's oilskin coat, widowed young, who has bought the Highsteads' wheat for twenty autumns and sold it at the Wendmouth fair. She drives the wagon herself, talks to the horses more than to people, and keeps her purse inside her coat.

**Voice.** Short sentences and farm sayings ("A wet road is still a road." "Bread doesn't argue."). Calls the Blades by their names once she has learned them, and "my lot" before that. Laughs at herself, never at the hill folk.

**Wants.** The wheat at the fair dry, whole and on time; her purse whole too. She would rather lose an hour than a sack, and a sack than a Blade.

**Knows and tells.** Every farm on the slopes, every ford on the river road, the price of a room at the Wendford alehouse. That the goblins have been seen on the ridges this year, closer than any year she remembers. That a toll-taker sometimes squats by the little bridge over the Millbeck, and the reeve never sent him.

**Hides.** Nothing about the contract. She is more frightened of the goblins than she lets on, and she has never seen a fight up close.

**On the cards.** On a caravan card she asks the Blades what they think, listens, and nearly always does what most of them say. She will not leave the wagon or its wheat behind for anyone; if a card offers it, she says no and says why.
