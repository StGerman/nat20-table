# Osric Hale, reeve of Wendford

**Who.** The village's headman for eleven years: stout, fifty, in a good brown coat he wears to look like the steward's equal. He keeps the tally sticks and lends from the common chest, and lately from his own purse.

**Voice.** Careful and a little pompous; he answers a question with "Well, now" and a pause. He calls the party "the company".

**Wants.** Wenna home, the wheel turning, and the village calm. He also wants the mill earning, because he paid for its new millstone this spring and Bertil can only repay him from the mill's takings.

**Knows and tells.** When the wheel stopped (two days ago, mid-morning), that Wenna went up alone because Bertil is laid up, the way to the mill. He offers payment from the common chest for her safe return.

**Hides.** The loan, which makes him look like he cares more for money than for the girl (Insight). That Abel the shepherd saw clawed tracks and big dog prints on the mill lane two days ago, which he kept quiet so the village wouldn't panic (Persuasion).

**Scenes.** 1 and 6.
