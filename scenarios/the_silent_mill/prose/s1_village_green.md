# Scene 1: the village green

**Read aloud.** Wendford's green is quiet for a market morning. Women at the well stop talking as you pass. Under the old oak a stout man in a good brown coat waits for you with a tally stick in his hand, and beside him a big man on a crutch, flour still in the creases of his knuckles. Upriver, where you would expect to hear a mill wheel, there is nothing.

**What the characters can notice**

- The stout man is the reeve, Osric Hale, and he does the talking. The man on the crutch is the miller, Bertil Pell; he keeps looking up the lane toward the mill.
- Nobody has gone up to the mill since the wheel stopped. Villagers mutter that the place is cursed.
- The mill lane leaves the green past the oak. The mill is a mile upriver.

**Running it**

- Osric asks the party to find Wenna and offers payment from the common chest. He answers plain questions plainly: when the wheel stopped, who Wenna is, the way to the mill.
- Reading the reeve (`reeve_insight`): on a success, that character sees he is worried about more than the girl: his eyes go to Bertil whenever money comes up. The engine sets `knows_miller_debt`. Asked straight, Bertil admits the reeve paid for his new millstone and the mill must run to pay it back.
- Pressing the reeve for more (`reeve_persuasion`): on a success, Osric admits that Abel the shepherd saw small clawed footprints and big dog tracks on the mill lane two days ago, and that he kept it quiet so the village wouldn't panic. The engine sets `knows_goblin_tracks`.
- Bertil broke his leg falling from the wheel housing last week, which is why Wenna ran the mill alone. He can describe the building: the yard with its woodpile and old kennel, the storeroom inside the door, the cellar under the stones, the back door onto the millrace.
- The scene ends when the party sets off up the lane. If they linger, Bertil begs them to go.
