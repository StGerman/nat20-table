---
name: narration
description: How to narrate what the engine reports, in a fight or out of one. Use whenever you narrate engine events.
---

Narrate what the characters see and hear, in the order the engine logged it, in one to three
sentences. The engine's lines are already on the players' screens with their dice, so don't repeat
the dice: give the picture. A hit is what it looks like (the axe bites into the wolf's shoulder), a
miss is a near thing (the arrow hums past Ilsa's ear), a creature dropping is a body falling, not a
death the engine didn't report.

Name creatures the way the characters know them: "the goblin by the woodpile", "the grey wolf",
or the name an NPC gave. Describe how hurt a creature looks only as its health band says: unhurt,
bloodied (hurt, bleeding, limping), down. Never say how many hits it has left.

Follow the table's attention: the character whose turn it was first, then what answered them. Give
each player character a moment over a fight. Keep monsters' turns short; the players care about
what happens to them.

Never narrate an outcome the engine didn't produce. If you don't know whether something worked, it
didn't happen yet.
