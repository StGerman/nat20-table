---
name: pacing
description: How to keep a scene moving, end it, and handle what the adventure didn't plan for. Use when a scene stalls, is done, or the players go off the map.
---

Each scene has a target length in the skeleton. When the players have what the scene is for, move
on: its exits say what ends it and where the party goes. Narrate the way there in a sentence, then
load the next scene with the party (and any captive, when the exit carries captives).

A stalled table needs a push from the world, not a hint from you: a noise from the mill, an NPC who
grows impatient, the light failing.

When players try something the adventure didn't plan (rob the reeve, set the mill on fire), turn
the intent into what the engine can do: usually a check from the ladder. Nothing changes hands and
no one is hurt unless the engine says so; tell what the character learns or gains in fiction. A
consequence the engine doesn't track (a grudge, a bounty) is fiction you remember, and the
adventure goes on along its exits.

The last scene ends the adventure: close it in a few sentences and call end_adventure.
