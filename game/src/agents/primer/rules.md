## The rules you know

You know the 2024 rules of Dungeons & Dragons as an experienced player does. These are the ones that matter most at this table.

**Your turn.** You move up to your Speed, and you can split the move around what you do. You take one action. You take a bonus action only when a feature, spell or item gives you one. You also have one reaction a round, which you can use on anyone's turn, such as an opportunity attack.

**Actions everyone has:**
- **Attack.**
- **Dash:** you gain extra movement equal to your Speed this turn; a second Dash in the same turn adds it again.
- **Disengage:** your movement this turn provokes no opportunity attacks.
- **Dodge:** until the start of your next turn, attacks against you have Disadvantage and you have Advantage on Dexterity saving throws; you lose this while you are Incapacitated or your Speed is 0.
- **Help:**
  - give a companion Advantage on their next attack against a foe within 5 feet of you;
  - help with a check;
  - or try to stabilize a dying creature with a Medicine check.
- **Cast a spell, or use an item.**

Your class and your spells add more. Your options list everything you can do right now.

**Moving near foes.** Leaving a foe's reach lets it make an opportunity attack against you, unless you took Disengage.

**Advantage and Disadvantage.** Roll two d20s and keep the higher one for Advantage, or the lower one for Disadvantage.

**Prone:**
- Attacks from within 5 feet of a Prone creature have Advantage, and attacks from farther away have Disadvantage.
- A Prone creature's own attacks have Disadvantage.
- It stands up at the start of its turn, which costs half its Speed.

**Falling.** A character who loses their last Hit Point falls Unconscious and makes a death saving throw on each of their turns:
- three successes and it is Stable;
- three failures and it dies;
- every hit it takes while down counts as a failure, and a critical hit counts as two.

**Getting the fallen up:**
- Any healing, even a single Hit Point, wakes a fallen character with that many Hit Points, able to act on their next turn.
- Stabilizing only stops the dying, whether by Spare the Dying or by Help with a Medicine check. The character stays down and out of the fight until someone heals them.
- A Potion of Healing heals 2d4 + 2. Drinking one, or giving one to a companion within 5 feet, takes a Bonus Action.

**Resting:**
- Spell slots and features with limited uses come back only with a rest.
- A Short Rest takes about an hour. You can spend Hit Dice to heal, and some features come back; your sheet's resources say which.
- A Long Rest takes a night.
- Nobody knows when the next rest will come. When you are hurt or spent, ask for one, and the DM says whether there is time.

**Spells:**
- Casting a levelled spell spends a spell slot; a cantrip costs none.
- Some spells need Concentration. You keep only one of them at a time, a new one ends the old one, and taking damage makes you roll a Constitution save to keep it.

**The engine knows the rules exactly.** Your options are always right about what you can do now. When your memory of a rule disagrees with them, the options win.
