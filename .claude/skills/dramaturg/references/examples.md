# Storytelling bible: Examples

The repository copy of the bible's Examples tab, for the current world. The rules are in `commandments.md` beside this file; where an example and a rule disagree, the rule wins. When the world is swapped, this file is replaced with it.

What each commandment looks like when it is done, taken from our own cards, people, and narration samples where one exists, and from a borrowed mechanism where it does not. The rule is in the tab Commandments and names nothing from here; this tab shows the rule done and what was cut. Everything in it belongs to the current world and is replaced with it. The reference narration today is sample F.

## World

Numbers follow the rules in Commandments. Card ids are from the deck *Faces of the Road* (`worlds/wend/deck/`), people from `worlds/wend/npcs.json`.

1. **Less is more.** The Millbeck bridge (`millbeck_bridge`) is a torn sheepskin coat, a borrowed helmet, and a notched tally stick: that is the whole toll. Who the two men are and why they stand there arrives only if the party asked the roadfolk the night before (`knew_of_the_toll_men`). The bog-wife (`bog_wifes_toll`) is a basket of knotted charms and a crow; no history, no name until she gives one. *Cut:* the gramophone (rejected by Stas): a technical anachronism, and it explained itself.
2. **Off the map until a story needs it.** The Hollow King exists as one line a peddler says over a healed hand (`tobins_hands`, deed `heard_of_the_hollow_king`); nothing else about him is written anywhere. Saltmouth's new money arrives before any Saltmouth person does: on the second trip the bridge has a painted board with a wax seal and a man with a ledger (`bridge_again`). The gods are known by kind and by rumour, never by name.
3. **A person is whoever can yield, be spoken to, and be remembered.** The two toll men can be talked down and later stand in the stocks on Wendford green (`toll_man_stocks`): persons, and the deck remembers them. The wolves on the water meadows (`night_wolves`) are weather with teeth: no name, no deed, no mercy owed. The Mill breaks this rule today (Gritch and the cellar band can only die); MAP-13 fixes it.
4. **Every people lives by its economy.** `customs.json` says it in one sentence each: valley folk share bread, pay a debt, keep a word, draw no blood inside the gate, and show mercy to one who yields; in Saltmouth a signed paper is the law; among the hill bands kin come before strangers; for the roadfolk the road is free. A card choice keeps or breaks one (`keeps: ["bread_shared"]` on *Share your food and listen*), and that is the whole ethics engine.
5. **Every person is right about something and ugly about something.** Osric Hale is right that nobody should take a toll in the reeve's name, and ugly in that he refused the hill farms the common chest and lost his niece to the hills for it. Fenn Lusk is right that a farm with no flock cannot repay salt on credit, and ugly in saying so to a widow across a cart of it, with the line that he too has three children and all of them eat (sample F). Col at the bridge is right about the fair price of grain leaving hungry hills (Thompson) and ugly in the borrowed helmet and the reeve's borrowed name.
6. **One detail you remember per person.** The boy's bobble hat sliding over his eyes (sample F); a goblin in a stretched tracksuit top; the toll man's helmet too big for him, which the silent pilgrim draws in the mud a day later (`what_agna_drew`) so the table recognises him by it. *Cut:* anything with a mechanism in it (a wristwatch, a gramophone, a lighter), and any detail the DM explains or calls odd.

## The compass

- **A people placed.** The Saltmouth houses live by trade (the Market columns) under a lord's charter (the Tribute rows): an outline over Market and Tribute, never a point. Fair face: the paper keeps the houses from eating each other, and a toll under a seal is at least a toll you can read (`bridge_again`, *Ask to read the paper*). Dark face: Rufus, whose coin comes back for the wagon at the fair gate (`rufus_offer`, `rufus_due`). The valley's nickname for them: paper men.
- **A people placed, the other way.** The roadfolk live by custom with nobody above the family: the Custom columns, the Freedom rows. Fair face: a fire with room for the Blades and the news of the road (`roadfolk_fire`). Dark face, as the valley sees it: a missing sack and a carter saying what the valley always says, that roadfolk were camped up the road (`missing_sack`). The deck lets the party find the sack on a grain dealer's stall instead (`oddas_mark`), which is the compass doing its work: the accusation was the valley's politics, not a fact.
- **A torn person.** Col and Dunn, two valley men, hold a Tribute cell nobody gave them: the reeve's name, a borrowed helmet, a tally stick. Through Thompson's eyes it is a fair-price action; through Osric's it is theft in his name; the stocks on Wendford green (`toll_man_stocks`) are where the two readings meet, and the party decides which one the reeve hears. Nell Hale's road from the valley outline to Web Faith is the same shape over a whole quest.
- *Cut:* a people that is only a fair face (the roadfolk as noble wanderers) or only a dark one (Saltmouth as villains); a faction placed at a single cell; a nickname nobody in the valley would say.
- **From the current grid.** The valley's names for its neighbours: paper men, tally-sticks, the lord's teeth. Cells nobody holds yet: Wizards' Council, Every Man a Lord. The company's four cells: Torva at Every Hearth a Field, Maren at Hearth Charity, Bren at Fed and Paid, Ilsa at Written Rights. A torn person: Dame Kell at Old Houses, leaning toward Salt Monopoly. A person who moves during a story: Nell's road from the valley outline to Web Faith. The drawn grid is in the tab Why.

## Story-space

The measure for every example here is a Vera reading: sample F is the reference narration, and her answers to samples A to F are in `/mnt/project-files/engine/bible/vera-feelings-2026-11-06.md`.

1. **Never a passenger.** The widow across the track (sample F): Maren lists the three ways and ends with «Решай»; nobody picks for the human. The fair price card: a crowd offers to buy the grain at the old price, and the human votes first (decision 0062). *Cut:* sample A, where Maren was already untying her purse and the human only had to nod.
2. **Every scene is a problem that resists.** A flock of black-faced sheep from wall to wall and no shepherd (`sheep_on_track`): no enemy, still a problem, and it costs time either way. Wolves on the water meadows (`night_wolves`): stand between them and the horses, or build the fire high, and the dog you fed yesterday lowers the odds of a fight (`if: fed_gwyns_dog`). *Cut:* a scene whose only door is a fight, or a scene that is a description with a question mark.
3. **A choice names the action, never its outcome, and no choice is free.** *Take them. The dead don't spend.* (`old_cairn`) names the act and the attitude; the deed it writes (`robbed_cairn`) and the pilgrim who will not take a seat from a cairn robber come later. *Throw them a sack of food and push on* costs four rations and keeps a custom; *Close ranks and fight* costs blood. *Cut:* "Persuade the guard (DC 15)", "Pay 20 copper and cross safely".
4. **The voice.** Sample F, 118 words: the flock killed in summer, Fenn's three children who all eat, the boy weighing your sword and deciding he could take it, four war veterans with nowhere to put their hands. Dark in what happens, plain in the telling, four jokes, nothing withheld. *Cut:* sample B's «Выше по склону лает собака. Одна.»: Vera read the withheld fact as a test she was failing, and stopped deciding.
5. **The DM narrates reactions, never verdicts, and never what the human's character feels.** Osric stops the Blades by the well: word came up the river that they paid a toll to men he never sent (`toll_paid_word`). He is angry; the DM does not say they were wrong. *Cut:* a sample that told Vera how she felt, and her reading of it: «странно, что мне рассказывают, что я чувствую».
6. **Funny in the people.** A sheepdog falls in beside the wagon as if it had been hired too (`stray_dog`); the roadfolk boy on a barrel at the fair gate telling a crowd how the Blades beat everything the road threw at them (`tams_story`); the veterans in front of the widow. The company is mocked for what it does. *Cut:* a joke about what the human's character feels, a repeated word pair, a joke that lands in one language only.
7. **Handrails.** Maren's line at the widow is the handrail: three ways, in plain words, before the question. Asked what the tracksuit top is, the DM answers inside the world ("it's a jacket"), never about it.

## Consequence

1. **Problems come from change, not scarcity.** The change is Saltmouth's paper, and it never appears as a person: a painted board with a wax seal where last trip two brothers held out a cap (`bridge_again`); a young man in a very good coat on a wagon with yellow wheels asking the grain cart to pull over (`yellow_wagon`); a dealer's stall at the fair with Odda's mark on two sacks (`oddas_mark`). In the Mill the change is the tribute the Hollow King began to take from the hills, and the goblins at the mill are its weather. *Cut:* a famine with no cause, a villain who explains the plan.
2. **Deeds come back.** Share your food with the peddler (`peddler`, deed `fed_tobin`) and he is the witness who puts his mark on a written account after the bridge (`peddlers_mark`), which the reeve reads at the ford (`osric_hears`, *Show him the peddler's mark*). Snub the bog-wife (`snubbed_sedge`) and when the horse goes lame there is no third way (`lame_horse` offers *Send back for the bog-wife* only `if: sedge_charm`). Only those who saw or heard react: Bel comes to the fire asking after her boy only because he rode (`bel_asks` reads `tam_rode`). The Mill writes no deeds yet, so its canon (Venna saved, Gritch dead, Snitt freed) rests on prose; gap A in the campaign line.
3. **A half-blind choice, a consequence on the young, something learned that goes stale.** Half blind: *Let him ride* (`boy_on_tailboard`) with no way to know his mother will come to the fire after dark. On the young: Tam on the barrel at the fair gate, a boy who got his story and may yet get his sword; in the Mill, Venna at the millstone; in Arachas, the sleepers in the cocoons under the oak. Gone stale: what the party learned about the toll on the first trip (two brothers, the reeve sent nobody) is wrong by the second, when the toll belongs to a company under a seal. *Cut:* a choice the DM explains the stakes of in full, a child who is a prop with no name.
4. **Losing is a consequence, never the end of the game.** *Let it go* (`missing_sack`): fifty of Odda's wheat are gone, she counts the sacks twice, and the contract goes on with less pay at the end. A failed Survival check on the moss costs rations, not the run. Death is final, by price and distance; only a story brings someone back. *Cut:* a game over screen, a reload.

## Guardrails

The lines in action, and the leaks that crossed them. Leaks teach more than the rule does.

1. **Only the engine rolls, reveals, and counts.** Done: "Twenty coppers" at the bridge because the `pay` effect said twenty, and the DM cites the roll as `[r14]`. *Leak:* "you needed a fifteen" (a residual in table #326): the DM named the target it should not know it knows. *Leak:* a DM who added two prices and said the sum; the number ban was removed only because the ledger now does that sum.
2. **The SRD is the world; ours only adds.** Done: the goblins at the mill are Goblin Warriors and a Goblin Boss; Arachas is a Giant Spider in a story that makes her older than memory. *Cut:* a homebrew monster where an SRD one with a different coat would do.
3. **Hidden values never reach a player.** Done: a card choice says *Tell him the reeve charges no such toll*, and the DC lives in the JSON. *Leak:* "Persuasion (DC 15)" in a choice text; a bare die roll on screen, which Vera read as «Семнадцать из скольки?».
4. **Torture and harm to a child are never shown.** Done: the sleepers under the oak are alive and asleep in their cocoons, told without naturalism; Venna is alive at the millstone. The threat to a child is told as what the party can prevent, never as what happened to the child.
5. **A player is never told they typed something wrong.** Done: an unparsed line gets the DM asking what the character does, in the world. *Leak:* "unknown command", «так нельзя».
6. **The Vera test runs on anything a player will see.** Done: six samples of one road moment, F chosen on her readings. *Cut:* a card shipped on the author's ear alone.
7. **A player's progress is never lost** (proposed). Done: the deed book and the purse carry over between contracts; the second trip reads the first (`bridge_again` reads `crossed_millbeck`).
8. **The DM never acts, speaks, or feels for the human's character** (proposed). Done: sample F gives the shuffling to the companions, not to the human. *Leak:* sample E, where the human's character «мнётся».
9. **We take mechanisms, never characters** (proposed). Done: a god with one believer left is small and poor (Small Gods), and ours has no tortoise; a failed roll makes the better scene (Disco Elysium), and ours has no voices in anyone's head. The table of what we take from whom is at the end of this tab.

## Building with them

One of each, built by the recipe in Commandments, from things that already exist in the deck.

- **A person: Col, the toll man.** Short name: Col. One want: the hill farm's winter. Speaks for: the hill farms the reeve refused. Right about: grain should not leave hungry hills at Saltmouth's price. Ugly about: he takes it in the reeve's name with a borrowed helmet. Holds: bread shared; bends: a word kept (the reeve sent nobody). One detail: the helmet too big for him, which the pilgrim draws in the mud. Can yield: yes (`toll_men_spared`), and the stocks on the green are what yielding costs him.
- **A place: the Millbeck bridge.** Who lives there and by which economy: nobody; valley folk cross it by custom, and the reeve has never taken a toll. Three details: a little bridge, a cap held out, a notched tally stick. One thing that changed, arrived from off the map: by the next trip a painted board with a wax seal and a man with a ledger. One problem at the door: a toll nobody sent, and then a toll somebody bought.
- **A story: the road to the fair.** A change the people cannot see: Saltmouth's paper reaching up the river. A problem that resists: the toll at the bridge. A choice with a cost on every side, made half blind: pay and be asked by the reeve why; talk them down and send them to the stocks; tell them you know why they stand there, if the roadfolk told you. A deed the world reads back: the reeve at the ford, or the dealer's stall with Odda's mark. A consequence for the young: Tam on the tailboard. One thing learned that goes out of date: whose toll it is.
- *Cut from each:* a backstory paragraph, a second want, a detail the DM has to explain.
