# The Wend valley

A quiet farming valley at the far eastern end of a small kingdom whose lord rarely looks this far. Written for *The Silent Mill*; *Fair in Wend* and *The Way Back* use it too, and other packs may.

## Places

- **The river Wend** runs south out of the Hollin Hills, slow and brown, and turns every mill in the valley. Above the mouth of the Millbeck the mill weirs and the fords close it to boats; below it, the Saltmouth boatmen pole flat barges between the Millbeck mouth and the town. A barge goes down with the current in half a day and comes back up only on a tow rope, with horses on the bank. A farmer with a wagon loads it once in the farmyard and drives it to the fair, which is cheaper than carting it to the river, loading it again and paying the boatmen.
- **Wendford** is a village of forty households where the old road crosses the river: a green with a well and an old oak, the reeve's house with its porch on the green, a shrine to Embra, sheep on the slopes. The market town is a day downriver.
- **Pell's mill** stands a mile upriver at the end of a hedged lane: a stone mill with an undershot wheel and a sluice gate above it, a cobbled yard with a woodpile and an old kennel, a storeroom inside the door, and a cellar under the stones where the grain is kept dry. A back door lets out onto the millrace.
- **The Hollin Hills** close the valley to the north: heather, bog and old barrows. Beyond them is nobody's land; the hills are the valley's edge, not a border.
- **The Highsteads** are upland farms eight miles up the slopes from Wendford, under the hills: wool, honey and peat. Above the farms stand the summer huts on the high pasture, where the families take the flocks from late spring to the first frost.
- **Bald Hill** (Лысая гора) is the bare hill above the Highsteads, on the path to the Barrow Oak. Col and Dunn's farm stands on its shoulder. The valley called it Bald Hill long before anyone now living, and people once lit fires on its top on certain nights; nobody has lit them in living memory, and nobody says why any more.
- **The Barrow Oak** is a hollow oak on a barrow at the valley's northern edge. The hill folk keep away from it, except to push a copper into its roots when they want something they cannot ask a person for.
- **Saltmouth** is the fair town and the valley's port, where the Wend meets the sea, twenty miles downriver from Wendford: a day with a wagon. It sells salt, iron and cloth, and people from every port pass through it. Its harbour is silting up: the old quay under the town walls stands on mud at low water, and ships now lie at an outer quay a mile down the shore, where the water is still deep; lighters carry their cargo up to the town, and a cart track runs along the shore to it. By the outer quay stands the caravanserai (караван-сарай), a house for anyone who pays a night: a square of walls round a yard with a well, stores and stables below, rooms above, one gate shut at dusk. Ships from far ports wait off a small island in the harbour mouth before anyone from them may land.
- **The old channel** is an arm of the river that leaves Saltmouth eastward to the sea. The road from town runs four miles east along its near bank, past the lord's old salt pans, to the old ferry landing, where the channel can be waded at low tide. Across it stand an empty customs house and the salt flats: pans, mud and creeks, walked at low tide. Three miles further east is the Dyke (Вал), an old earth bank that runs inland from the sea along the far edge of the flats and ends where they do, and past it the marshes.
- **The old road** runs west and east through Wendford. West it goes into the kingdom; east of the valley it leaves the kingdom across the Free Host's country. The caravans that use it come from far cities the valley rarely sees.

## The kingdom and its border

- The kingdom is small: a strip of coast and the valleys that run down to it. Its lord holds court in a harbour town about a day's sail west along the coast, which the valley has never seen.
- Past the Dyke, on the marshes, live the **Free Host** (Вольница): free fighting men and their families under no lord. They keep a hard faith of their own, choose their captain in a circle, and bind themselves by oath, not by paper. They take in anyone who comes to them, clothe and arm them, and never hand them back: a runaway's debts do not follow him into their country. The Free Host are not the lord's subjects and owe him nothing.
- The border runs from the sea northward along the edge of the marshes, east of the valley, to the foot of the hills. Until last winter its last stretch, by the sea, was the old channel, and the salt flats across it were the Free Host's; the customs house on the far bank was theirs, where they asked who was crossing and took a ferry toll, never a duty on salt. The lord's border war was fought along the whole of it, mostly at breaches and walls inland, far from the flats.
- The peace was not drawn by either side. Both called in the **Wizards' Council** (Совет магов), a court and parliament of mages that sits in the trading cities over the sea, judges quarrels between lands and makes the laws for magic. The Council drew the new line on its map without setting foot on the flats: the flats went to the lord, and the border's last stretch moved from the old channel to the Dyke. The Free Host's captain swore to it before the Council. The Council's surveyors are setting stones along the Dyke, each cut with its mark; stones set by day are often gone by morning, and Saltmouth blames the Free Host. The ferry stopped, and the customs house stands empty.
- The battle-mages of the war fought under the Council's watch: what a mage may do in a war is the Council's law, not the lord's.

## Salt and Saltmouth's treasury

- Salt pays for Saltmouth. The old salt house holds the lord's charter for the old pans on the near bank, and the duty on every sack sold under it pays for the town's walls, the fair warden and the lighters to the outer quay. As the harbour silts and the outer quay costs more, the duty goes up: this is why salt is dear this year. Nobody up the valley knows it; they only see the price. Saltmouth's dealers know and keep it to themselves. No pack explains it on screen unless the pack says so.
- The charter does not name the new ground across the channel, so the pans there are worked outside it, and past the Dyke salt carries no duty at all. Salt that crosses the flats at low tide is a hole in the town's treasury, and the town has every reason to watch the flats.

## The sea

- From Saltmouth ships go west along the coast to the lord's harbour, south to small islands that belong to nobody and grow rich whenever a coast is closed to trade, and over the sea to the trading cities where the Wizards' Council sits. None of this is on the map yet.

## People and customs

- A **reeve** is a village's headman, chosen by the households and answerable to the lord's steward, who visits once a year. The reeve keeps the tally sticks, settles small quarrels and lends from the common chest.
- **Embra, the Hearth Mother**, is the valley's goddess of home fires, bread and mercy. Her sign is a round loaf scored with a cross. Her priests are expected to feed the hungry and to spare an enemy who yields.
- Most coin in the valley is silver pennies, with copper for small things; gold is merchants' money: it comes up the river in their purses and goes out as the pay of the Hired Blades they hire. A mill is the richest thing a village owns after its land, and a stopped one is felt in every kitchen within a week.

## The goblins of the hills

- Goblin bands are small, hungry and quarrelsome, each led by the strongest goblin that can keep it. They keep wolves for hunting and for war, and they speak Goblin among themselves; a few know enough Common to bargain.
- The **Nettle Tooth** band stains its teeth green with nettle juice. Down the valley it is known for stealing sheep, not for raiding farms.
- This autumn the bands have been moving south out of the hills. Hill folk say something new rules the barrows, something the goblins call **the Hollow King**, and that it takes tribute from every band. Nobody in Wendford knows more than the rumour.

## The Grey Lantern company

The four pregenerated characters served together in the Grey Lantern company, hired soldiers paid off last winter when the lord's border war ended. They fought at the breaches, far from the salt flats: they heard the border moved to the Dyke past the flats, but they have never been there and could not say where the line runs on the ground. They have travelled together since, taking guard work where they find it, and they reach Wendford on the morning the adventure begins.
